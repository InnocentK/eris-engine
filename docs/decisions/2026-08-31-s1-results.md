## Findings the predictions did not ask about

### S1-A. The verify step produced nothing, in every judged cell

`verify.refused` 11, successful verify executions **0**. Every command the
models proposed was refused by the allowlist:

| refusals | reason |
|---|---|
| 4 | `command 'python3' is not allowlisted` |
| 4 | `git subcommand must be one of [blame, diff, grep, log, ls-files, rev-parse, show, status]` |
| 1 | `argv[2] contains a control character` |
| 1 | `command 'ls' is not allowlisted` |
| 1 | `command 'grep' is not allowlisted` |

The allowlist is behaving exactly as designed - this is the control working, not
failing. But the consequence for the experiment is direct: **`indep_verify`'s
verification stage contributed zero evidence in all four cells**, and the
adjudicator ruled on unverified claims. Whatever arm 3 measures here, it is not
"proposal plus independent mechanical verification".

The same pattern appeared in S0, where the claude proposer asked for
`python3 -m py_compile`, `python3 -c ...` and `git show HEAD:README.md`. Models
consistently request the commands a reviewer would actually run, and the
allowlist consistently refuses them. That gap is a design question for Phase 0,
not a defect to patch mid-campaign.

### S1-B. Cache reuse is unavailable to exactly the two missing-cost workers

`cache.refused: "entry has no model_executed"` - for codex and agy, in every
`indep_verify` cell. claude and grok were reused all four times; codex and agy
re-executed all four times.

The cache stores an entry it will not later reuse, because it cannot confirm the
same model produced it: codex and agy envelopes carry no model id (`agy`'s policy
comment already said "NOT observed executing"). The guard is right. Its
distribution is unfortunate:

* **Cost.** The workers that cannot be cached are the workers charged $2 per
  call. 8 of the 17 missing-cost calls in S1 are re-executions that claude and
  grok did not have to pay.
* **Pairing.** The plan assumed arm 1 and arm 3 share proposals. They share
  claude's and grok's - byte-identical replays - and not codex's or agy's, which
  are fresh draws. `cache.conflict` fired 8 times, recording that the re-run
  output differs from the stored entry. So an arm-to-arm comparison on this
  corpus is **paired for two workers and unpaired for two**, and any difference
  attributed to the arm is partly proposal variance for half the panel.

This is the sharpest methodological finding of the run and it was invisible from
the code: the cache path, the pin path and the cost path each behave correctly
in isolation.

### S1-C. A timeout costs $2, like an interrupt

`plan-migration-r0-single-agy`: `stage.started` 15:24:35, `retry.scheduled`
15:34:08 (~573 s, against agy's 600 s timeout), completed on attempt 2 at
15:35:35. agy therefore has 9 cost records for 8 successful executions. Same
mechanism as S0's interrupt: a call that never returns a cost books the full
`per_call_usd`. Retries are not in the sizing table.

### S1-D. `wrong_accept` fired, and discriminates

`plan-migration-r0-indep_verify` returned **ACCEPT** where the key requires
REVISE - `wrong_accept: true`. `plan-rollout-r0-indep_verify` returned ESCALATE -
not a wrong accept. So `wrong_accept_rate` for `indep_verify` on plan tasks is
**0.5 (1 of 2)**, and 0.0 for every unjudged arm, structurally, as the split
record predicted.

Worth stating beside it: on the two CONTROL tasks, whose expected verdict is
ACCEPT, `indep_verify` returned REVISE and ESCALATE. The judged arm was wrong in
both directions on this corpus, and `wrong_accept` by construction sees only one
of them. That is the metric doing its job, not a hidden failure - but a report
that quotes `wrong_accept_rate` alone would describe an arm that never said
"revise this clean code", which it did, twice.

### S1-E. Precision is the number that separates the arms

Recall on plan tasks clusters (0.6, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2, 0.0); precision does not.
`indep_verify` reaches 0.6 recall at **0.1 precision** - 11 and 16 false
positives on the two plan cells, and 5 redundant predictions collapsed by the
coverage rule. `single:claude` reaches the same 0.6 recall at 0.25 precision
with 1 redundant. An arm that says everything scores well on recall alone, which
is why the pre-registered rule pairs catch rate with `wrong_accept_rate` and why
neither should be read on its own.
