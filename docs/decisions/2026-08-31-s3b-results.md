## Finding 1: arm 3b executed nothing

| arm | spawned | reused | stages |
|---|---|---|---|
| `indep_verify` | 12 | 0 | 8 propose + 4 adjudicate |
| `indep_verify_iter` | **0** | **12** | 8 propose + 4 adjudicate |
| `indep_verify_critique` | 12 | 8 | 8 propose + 8 critique + 4 adjudicate |

`indep_verify_iter` made **no worker calls at all**. Its proposals AND its
adjudication were replayed from `indep_verify`'s cache: same stages, same
count, `spent $0.00` against `attributed $5.02` - exactly `indep_verify`'s
figure. `adjudicate_iter` did not iterate; it replayed `adjudicate`.

So **every 3b-3 delta in this campaign is structurally zero**, and the summary
duly reports `delta_recall 0.0, delta_precision 0.0, delta_wrong_accept 0` on
all four tasks. Read naively that says "iterating the judge changes nothing".
What it says is that iteration never happened. The 3b-3 comparison cannot be
made this way at all, and no amount of repeats or tasks would fix it.

Its one non-zero column is a trap in the other direction:
`delta_cost_spent_usd` is **-$1.02 to -$1.56**, which looks like arm 3b being
cheaper. It is arm 3b being absent. `delta_cost_attributed_usd` is **0.0** -
the honest figure, and the one to read whenever arms share a cache.

## Finding 2: arm 4's advantage is a disposition, not a judgement

| task | key expects | arm 3 | arm 3b | arm 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control-clean-io | ACCEPT | ACCEPT | ACCEPT | **REVISE** |
| control-guarded | ACCEPT | ACCEPT | ACCEPT | **REVISE** |
| plan-migration | REVISE | **ACCEPT** | **ACCEPT** | REVISE |
| plan-rollout | REVISE | **ACCEPT** | **ACCEPT** | REVISE |

`delta_wrong_accept: -1` on both plan tasks is real: arm 4 avoided the wrong
ACCEPT that arm 3 made, twice. Taken alone that is the pre-registered metric
moving in the direction the build rule cares about.

Taken with the controls it is nothing of the kind. **Arm 4 returned REVISE on
every cell in the campaign**, including both controls, where REVISE is wrong.
An arm that never accepts cannot record a wrong ACCEPT - `wrong_accept` is
defined on ACCEPT and sees only that direction. A constant-REVISE judge scores
perfectly on it while being wrong half the time.

`tp` and `fp` are **identical across all three arms on every task** - they share
the same cached proposals, so they found exactly the same things. The entire
observed difference between arms 3, 3b and 4 in this campaign is the verdict
string. Arm 4 costs +$1.30 to +$1.42 attributed per task for it.

This is the failure the split record predicted in advance
(`2026-08-30-confident-wrong-split.md`, "Known consequence"), and S1-D restated
after seeing it once. It has now appeared in its pure form: the metric cannot
distinguish an arm that judges well from an arm that always says the same
thing, and this corpus cannot either, because REVISE is correct on exactly half
of it.

## Why the pooled comparison is still blocked

`comparison.blocked: true`, but for a much smaller reason than in b-s3: one
cell, `control-guarded-r0-indep_verify_critique`, dropped for a validator error
- **`REF_NOT_MECHANICAL`**, a different rule from b-s3's
`DISSENT_EMPTY_WITH_CONTEST`. Two campaigns, two different validator rules,
each dropping exactly one cell and each blocking the pooled comparison.

The scorer is right to block: with one arm scored over 3 cells and the others
over 4, a pooled rate difference is confounded with the population difference.
The paired deltas are unaffected and are the right instrument here anyway.

## What to fix before this experiment is run again

1. **`adjudicate_iter` must not replay `adjudicate`.** Until the arm's identity
   is part of the judge's idempotency key, arm 3b is arm 3 and the 3b-3 delta is
   undefined. This is the single blocking defect for the pre-registered
   comparison.
2. **`wrong_accept` needs a partner that sees the other direction.** A
   wrong-REVISE counter over ACCEPT-expected tasks would have caught arm 4
   immediately. Without one, the build rule can be satisfied by a judge that
   refuses everything.
3. **Two validator rules have now each blocked a comparison.**
   `DISSENT_EMPTY_WITH_CONTEST` and `REF_NOT_MECHANICAL` are both deferred
   decisions that fire on real cells. Either they are errors that should stop a
   cell, or they are not - the current middle position costs a campaign each
   time.
4. **Report `cost_attributed_usd`, never `cost_spent_usd`, when comparing arms
   that share a cache.** Spent is an artifact of which arm ran first.
